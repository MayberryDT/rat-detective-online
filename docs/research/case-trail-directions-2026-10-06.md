## Distinct alternatives

Numbers below are proposed initial caps, not measured optima. Every direction vanishes during results, spectators’ non-game views and death recap; it returns from current state on respawn/welcome. Carrying switches the guidance slot to the existing scoring destination (none for Excessive Force).

| Direction | Exactly what the player does/sees | Budget and multiplayer consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Follow the paperwork**: player-relative route | On spawn, two small flat paper-chevron marks appear on walkable ground ahead: one on the nearby route, one at its next bend. Moving advances these two marks toward the case. They never require stepping on them. At a floor change, the second mark sits at the usable entrance/stair and includes ↑/↓. On reaching direct sight within a proposed 12 units, route marks disappear and a small steady briefcase glyph identifies the objective. Shoot, take/disarm, or take another route. | Maximum **two marks OR one fallback lead**, never both, no full-city line, particles, glow, rings, historical litter or clues to read. Each player sees their own approach to the same case. Best path fit; route correctness is the main implementation risk. “Follow the paperwork” is flavor, not deduction. |
| **B — Spilled evidence**: actual case history | A moving case leaves up to three monochrome paper slips at the most recent ground-supported positions, with folded corners pointing along travel. An always-available edge briefcase arrow leads toward the current case so players never have to search for the trail’s beginning. A stationary/respawned case has one slip beside it, not a fabricated travel history. On handoff/drop/reset, previous ownership history clears. | Maximum **three slips and one lead**, only within a proposed 20 units, never every rat’s footprints. This communicates real movement and allows optional route reading; it risks trailing a faster carrier, false continuity across roofs and extra clutter. Direction is reliable only because of the lead. Genuine inference is optional and weak; making it necessary violates this brief. Least suitable first experiment. |
| **C — One lead**: screen-space directional clue | One small paper-tab/briefcase arrow stays at a reserved screen margin and continuously points toward the case. It may say only CASE · ↑/↓ when on another level. It becomes a steady small glyph over the case when visible nearby. Player chooses streets, flanks and shortcuts and keeps firing. | Maximum **one cue**, no ground marks. Strongest clutter benchmark, cheapest bounded candidate. It gives bearing, not a route around walls; elevation means “case above”, not “stairs here”. Detective flavor comes from the paper tab and wording, not investigative decisions. |

**Why A over B:** lead players into the present fight rather than along a carrier’s past. A’s route is helpful visual navigation, not a claim that the carrier really dropped papers there. Do not animate pages blowing toward hidden bodies or name the route “evidence of passage”. Route following should be skippable; interceptions and supply detours remain viable.

**Why retain C as a real contender:** if routing cannot be correct cheaply, the smaller honest bearing cue is preferable to a misleading trail. It satisfies always-available guidance, though not the stronger walkable-path preference.

### Edge cases that decide viability

| Situation | A: short route | B: spilled history | C: one lead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moving/shot/airborne case | Replan boundedly; during flight point to current airborne position with one AIR cue, no invented ground path to a predicted landing. Resume route when grounded. | No prints through air; break history, current lead stays. | Bearing follows presented current case; AIR when necessary. |
| Occlusion/dense fight | Only supported visible route marks; a wall never receives fake floor marks. Fall back to one lead if marks unavailable. | Slips can hide naturally; current lead must carry acquisition. | Remains legible but cannot resolve a blocking building. |
| Elevation/sewers/docks | Route through real safe portals; ↑/↓ only at a verified transition. No route over water or through locked geometry. | Break trail between levels; slips must not be projected onto wrong floor. | Clearly indicate different level; no implication of traversability. |
| Handoff/return/reset/respawn | Clear cached goal/marks immediately and rebuild; no old trail to previous carrier. | Delete old slips; late-joining clients cannot infer missing history as “no movement”. | Replace target immediately; behind-camera rotation must not flip. |
| Color blindness/Blackout/low graphics | Pale fill, dark border and asymmetric chevron/paper shape; no dependency on red, bloom, motion or dynamic lighting. | Same contrast/shape rule; no footprint color code. | Shape and text redundant; identical low-graphics information. |
| Mobile | Two marks remain above joystick obscuration; fallback lead placed clear of both thumbs, score and briefing. | Three distant slips may be too small; reject rather than adding more. | Single fixed safe slot; proposed 24–32 px glyph, no tap required. |

Do not place the cue over the crosshair or a combatant’s torso. Never turn the gameplay camera toward the guidance automatically; players keep their aim while moving toward or flanking the case. Case versus destination uses briefcase versus building/zone shape, not color alone. Remove competing case cues instead of dimming a stack of them. Route marks should be steady; optional transition fades respect reduced motion. No new directional audio: firing and danger sounds remain available.

### Final source-gap check and recommendation

Fresh reads on 6 October 2026 closed the useful remaining gaps:

- **Bearing versus route:** Matt Vainio, Sucker Punch’s lead VFX artist, explicitly describes pointing the wind at the objective rather than routing around obstacles in [How stunning visual effects bring Ghost of Tsushima to life](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=345372), published 12 January 2021; fetched page also displays 1 May 2026 metadata, whose meaning is not assumed. This is direct developer evidence for C/D’s limitation. Do not call the wind a pathfinder. [Parker Hamilton’s GDC 2021 session page](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027399/Exploration-in-Ghost-of-Tsushima) corroborates its exploration/fantasy intent; only the session metadata/abstract was read, not its talk.
- **Camera interference:** [EA’s Dead Space PC gameplay settings](https://www.ea.com/able/resources/dead-space/dead-space/pc/gameplay), undated page, says the optional Orient View to Locator Path rotates the camera when the locator is used. Reject automatic camera rotation here: that is a proposal based on our shooter priorities, not a demonstrated defect in Dead Space.
- **Visible path precedent:** [Prima Games’ Dead Space 2 chapter-one guide](https://primagames.com/eguides/dead-space-2-eguide/walkthrough/clarkes-log-walkthrough/chapter-1), undated hosted guide for the 2011 game, describes a blue holographic pathway toward a destination and optional detours. This is a secondary gameplay reference, below EA’s primary documentation in authority. It supports the visible-route comparison, not exact current remake rendering or a proved multiplayer benefit. No unsupported ground-line geometry claim is needed.
- **Contrast:** [Game Accessibility Guidelines’ contrast guidance](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/provide-high-contrast-between-text-ui-and-background/), undated collaborative guidance, recommends a plain contrasting background or outlines/shadows. For our labels use that principle; a simple palette/grayscale pass does not certify real gameplay readability. The page’s 4.5:1 text/UI recommendation is not asserted as a validated threshold for tiny perspective ground marks.
- **Motion:** [Microsoft XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) explains distraction and readability barriers from moving/blinking UI and recommends ways to disable/hide it. It also explicitly excludes ancillary gameplay from part of its UI criteria. Our steady, non-pulsing cue and reduced-motion fallback are design choices informed by it, not a claim of formal XAG compliance or a ban on normal projectiles. This weakens D’s priority relative to C.
- **GDC date:** [Overwatch session metadata](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023317/Overwatch-The-Elusive-GoalPlay) was freshly opened to qualify the 2016 authored slide deck; the misleading extracted PDF title remains excluded.

**Trustworthy recommendation:** A is the preferred *design direction* for Tyler’s requested path with detective flavor. C is the lower-complexity comparison and fallback. Neither has human clarity evidence yet. Use A only if its two steady route marks reliably select a usable next turn/floor transition without new routing machinery becoming the project. Replace the two marks with one bearing lead when unavailable; never stack them. If that narrow routing requirement cannot be met, choose C honestly and leave floor/obstacle decisions to players. Park B (history can lead behind the fight) and D (motion duplicates C’s information). These are reasoned tradeoffs, not measured rankings.

Do not sell this as “players become detectives through deduction”: paper design, cuff/briefcase identity and concise wording supply detective flavor. Players’ meaningful decisions remain whom to shoot, how to approach, when to flank/disarm and which pickups to take. The exact shared objective-knowledge change remains the main gameplay tradeoff requiring owner choice; visual style alone cannot resolve it.
