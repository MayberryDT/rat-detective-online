## Findings from the current source

`CaseFiles.ts` repeats the exact same two-sheet geometry, five line marks, and shallow symmetric V crease at every clue. Only yaw and uniform scale vary. There is no paper artwork, grain, edge wear, or document variation. `caseClues.ts` places evidence on navigation-grid points every four units and adds cardinal neighbors around each rat: both the repeated cluster and regular arrangement read as markers rather than dropped paperwork. Positions are floor height plus .08, without a surface normal, so sloping ground is another grounding risk.

Flicker has multiple plausible mechanisms, not a confirmed single root cause. Paper, ink and rim are separate surfaces separated by .003/.004 units, with overlapping sheets only .012 units apart. Thin .009 ink and .012 rim geometry can alias at distance; intersecting/near-coplanar surfaces can produce depth artifacts. The camera uses near .1/far600 and automatic render resolution can fall to .7. `CaseFiles.update` also hard-culls a whole cluster from its center's frustum and one camera-to-point physics ray in `GameSession`, so partial visibility at a corner can pop. Shared paper positions are rebuilt from moving route windows, creating a separate source of appearing/disappearing papers. These mechanisms are evidenced in source; the precise symptom Tyler saw still needs a short instrumented moving-camera capture to distinguish them. Earlier screenshot checks did not establish temporal stability or aesthetic acceptance.

## Recommended native implementation

1. **Isolate the flicker first.** Record standing still, slow camera pan, walking past a corner and a moving case. Record only clue IDs/positions/visibility reasons alongside the clip. For a local diagnostic comparison, hold the clue list fixed, then bypass center-ray culling while retaining GPU depth testing. This separates actual list changes from culling and raster shimmer. No live deployment for this diagnosis.
2. **Create six genuinely different noir documents.** A typed statement, carbon-copy form, narrow receipt, notebook scrap, folded letter and torn report. Mostly single sheets, occasionally a deliberate nonintersecting pair. Warm off-white and grey stock, uneven charcoal typewriter ink, restrained creases/stains, a few clipped or torn corners. Most lie flat, with occasional slight asymmetric folds/curls. Keep the existing world scale approximately, judging against the rat's paw and real shoulder camera. No glowing sheet, thick border, identical V folds or arbitrary flapping.
3. **Use a shared texture atlas and simple meshes.** Paint six distinct document designs once using Canvas2D and upload a `CanvasTexture`, as existing scenery already does. Bake paper, ink and thin red perimeter into the same surface/material rather than stacking separate ink and border geometry. Use `MeshStandardMaterial` for diffuse rough paper; if the outline needs a visibility floor, use a red-only emissive mask on that same material, with no bloom. A handful of low-poly geometry variants each use the shared atlas and one instanced batch: target six paper draws, shared textures/material, no per-sheet lights, physics or new dependency. Warm/dispose through existing lifecycle. Normal depth testing keeps everything behind walls hidden.
4. **Filter for the actual camera.** Mipmaps, linear filtering and capped anisotropy for oblique ground views, with padded atlas tiles to avoid bleeding. Keep document marks broad enough to read as print without requiring readable text. If cutout edges are used, alphaTest plus alphaToCoverage is available because the renderer requests MSAA; verify it at reduced render scale. Avoid alphaHash noise or ordinary alpha-blended overlapping sheets. Use polygon offset only for demonstrated ground depth conflict, not as a substitute for nonintersecting geometry. A decal can fit completely flat litter but is not the default: a small sheet mesh can fold and use the same render path everywhere.
5. **Make placement stable and irregular.** Keep authoritative traversable routes and guaranteed nearby spawn leads. Choose art/shape/rotation/tint once from a stable clue ID. Replace symmetric four-way spills with small asymmetric groups; vary spacing and lateral offset within the verified walkable corridor. Reprobe footprints/support and slope before placing; do not randomize papers into walls or off ramps. Keep unchanged route segments anchored instead of reseeding them as rats move. Separate route validity from visual selection; preserve current evidence correctness when the case relocates. Let GPU depth handle partial wall occlusion rather than removing a whole visible sheet based on its center. Use bounds-aware frustum selection and stable budget/range boundaries. Keep bot observations sight-tested separately.
6. **Show a small in-game sample before replacing staging.** Compare six props in one street location at the actual shoulder camera, then walk a short trail through a corner and sewer ramp. Provide a repeatable 20–30 second clip and close/normal-distance stills, at native and .7 render scale, under a streetlamp and during Blackout. Pass criteria: varied documents read as paper, red edge is quiet but identifiable, no surface crawling or whole-clue flicker with a fixed state, papers sit on supported ground, walls hide them, and nearby evidence exists on fresh spawn/reconnect. Record draw counts, frame timing and texture memory against current build. Tyler judges the visual direction from that sample; a passing data/browser assertion alone is insufficient.

## Scope and order

First deliverable is the flicker diagnosis plus the six-prop in-game sample and motion clip. Then integrate stable route placement and verify spawn/case tracking. Do not deploy another visual guess to staging before the sample has been reviewed. Keep the existing route mechanics, case pickup authority, bot equality and human-only room lifecycle. No unit tests or new rendering framework are needed; use the existing real-browser E2E and visual artifacts.

## Primary references inspected

- https://threejs.org/docs/pages/CanvasTexture.html — built-in canvas-backed texture upload.
- https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html — native lit paper material, maps, roughness and emissive-map support.
- https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Texture.html — mipmaps, linear minification, anisotropy and color-space handling.
- https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Material.html — depth testing, polygon offset, alphaTest, MSAA alphaToCoverage; alphaHash explicitly introduces noise.
- https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html — shared geometry/material instances and per-instance transforms/colors.
- https://threejs.org/docs/pages/DecalGeometry.html and https://threejs.org/examples/webgl_decals.html — surface-projected decals considered for flat litter, not selected for the primary paper props.

Local precedents: `KitArchitecture.ts`, `HarbourWater.ts`, `ParkedVehicles.ts` use CanvasTexture; `createGame.ts` enables MSAA; `graphicsQuality.ts` allows .7 render scale; `NoirCity.ts` defines actual material patching. Merely clearing noNoir on a later-created object does not prove it joined the initial city-material collection; confirm the existing dynamic-material integration explicitly in the sample.
