## Preserve the game

- **No time limits on game modes, ever (Tyler, 1 October: "a hard rule").** No round clock, deadline, overtime, sudden death or elapsed-time win in any mode. Timers are allowed only for short boosts and power-ups, or when a player directly controls them (as Closing Time's holder did). Round length comes from gameplay: tune it with mechanics, never with a clock. Excessive Force stays at **10 kills with the case**; other modes may raise their targets if mechanics make them too short.
- **Bots are equal participants (Tyler, 30 September).** A bot has exactly the tools and abilities a human has: one shared rat body driven by the same controls a player produces (move keys, look, jump, fire), with shared speed, acceleration, jump, air control, Hot Pursuit, hitbox and fire limit. Never add bot-only movement rules, limits or copied constants. Only the mind and skill (`SkillDials`: reaction, aim error, crosshair turn speed) differ. Measure everything the same way for humans and bots, inputs included. See [the bot overhaul plan](docs/bot-overhaul.md).
- **Bot decisions and pickups (Tyler, 30 September).** Humans decide rarely: Jev is asked on events (spawn, goal ends, case changes) and at most every 10 s otherwise, and each answer sets a goal and a fight-or-ignore stance that the bot holds until the next decision. Humans never run past a usable pickup: a bot takes every stocked supply it can see within 12 units on its floor, whatever its goal and even carrying the case, except a Quick Fix at full health; held buffs refresh (the reflex, L2 of [the bot learning plan](docs/bot-learning-plan.md), `mindVersion` 5). This supersedes the September 12 and 13 on-route pickup limits below. Every change to play gets a before-and-after era comparison ([the data plan](docs/data-plan.md): every fact carries `build`; agent browsers join with `agent=1` and never count as human or switch Jev on). Tyler freezes one build for about a month of Jev data, with code-only rounds, before his later changes reach production.
